# Measuring a mechanism keyword before adding it

Over **7,727,217 indexed titles and abstracts**, up to 200 records per candidate drawn uniformly from its pool. NOT the top 200 by rank: ranking by title weight and then scoring whether the title carries the concept is circular, and the first version of this page did exactly that.

## Does the measure discriminate?

**Not decidably.** Every real candidate does score above every control (27% against 20%), so the ordering is right, but the gap is 7% against a standard error of 4.4% -- under two SE, so it is not separable from sampling noise.

Both ends are binomial estimates over small samples: `magnetotactic bacteria` at 27% over 168 records against `helicobacter pylori` at 20% over 200. Reading the `>` alone would announce a calibrated measure on a narrow gap between two noisy numbers, which is how a measure gets trusted for the wrong reason. Treat the verdicts below as ordered but not validated, and note that the candidate establishing the lower bound is itself REFUSED by the 30% floor.

## What this does not establish

**The taxonomy is unchanged by this page either way.** A phrase marked `admit` has cleared a proxy at a stated floor, which is a reason to read the sample, not a decision to tag articles with it. Admission is a separate change with its own audit, because the keyword this instrument exists to prevent -- `membrane potential`, 13.3% precision over 182 articles -- would have cleared a floor like this one too.

Title aboutness is a PROXY for topical precision, not a measurement of it. A paper can be about a mechanism its title does not name, and a title can name a concept the paper only surveys. Precision is decided by reading, so a deterministic sample of 10 records per candidate is written to `mechanism-keyword-sample.tsv` with an empty verdict column; nothing here fills it in.
