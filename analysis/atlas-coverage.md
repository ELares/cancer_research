# The frozen corpus measured against the cancer census (#ATLAS)

Generated by `scripts/atlas_coverage.py`. Read-only.

**Census: 1590/1334 baseline files (119.2%), 4,403,994 cancer articles.**

PubMed reports 4,276,707 articles under `neoplasms[mh]`. The C04 pass landed at
4,203,236, i.e. 98.3% of that, which is the check that the local MeSH filter
reproduces NLM's own tree explosion. The census EXCEEDS the C04 reference
because the definition was then extended past tree C04 with nine adjacent
experimental-context descriptors -- so it should not be read as agreeing with,
or overshooting, `neoplasms[mh]`. See `analysis/atlas-README.md`.

> **Per-mechanism capture is conditioned on having a tag.** The mechanism layer reaches 6.80% of the cancer literature measured through the PRODUCTION matcher (the 5.98% this sentence used to quote was a raw keyword loop over title and abstract, not what production reads). And the capture spread here is NOT variation inside that fraction: it is computed by the MeSH leaf map on both sides, a different instrument. See `analysis/atlas-taxonomy-reach.md` (#730), which retracts both halves of the earlier sentence.

The PMC share UPPER-BOUNDS any full-text analysis; it is not the amount of text
actually obtainable. A PMC identifier is necessary but not sufficient for an
article to be in the redistributable open-access subset, and the actual pull
retrieved fewer. The frozen corpus is 98.7% open access, which is not what the
literature looks like.

## What the census says about the frozen corpus

1,171 of 4,830 frozen records (24.2%) are absent from the
census. Splitting them is informative, because the two causes point in opposite
directions:

| cause | n | share of frozen corpus |
|---|---|---|
| not yet MeSH-indexed (recent, concentrated 2022-2026) | 940 | 19.5% |
| indexed by NLM, but under no C04 descriptor | 231 | 4.8% |

The first is the census's blind spot and is recovered by
`scripts/atlas_unindexed.py`.

The second cuts the other way and is a limit of the C04 definition itself: of
those 231, **186 (81%) are cancer papers by text**, which NLM
indexed under other trees (drug, enzyme, method) with cancer only as application
context. So a pure C04 filter loses roughly
3.9% of a hand-built cancer corpus. Neither a keyword
corpus nor a MeSH census is a superset of the other.

The residue -- **45 records (0.9%)** -- is genuinely not cancer:
pulsed-electromagnetic-field studies of bone healing and pain, mRNA vaccines for
COVID/Shigella/chikungunya, CRISPR method papers, bioelectric developmental
biology.

**45 of those 45 (100%) carry no mechanism tag at all.** That
matters for attributing blame: the mechanism tagger correctly declined to tag
them, so this is a QUERY-level defect in `scripts/queries.txt` pulling non-cancer
literature, distinct from the tag-level precision problem measured in
`analysis/mechanism-precision-report.md`. The two defects share themes (PEMF,
vaccines, CRISPR) but they are separate and need separate fixes.


## What this changes

1. Every mechanism count in the manuscript is a numerator without a
   denominator. The census supplies it, and the ratios can finally be stated
   as coverage rather than as volume.
2. Topics absent from the 19 queries are not small; they were simply never
   asked for. The table above is the first measurement of their size.
3. The open-access share of the census bounds every full-text claim the
   project can ever make, and it is far below the frozen corpus's 98.7%.
