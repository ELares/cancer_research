# The penetration headline is measured where lambda cancels (#926)

Generated by `scripts/penetration_access_vs_depth.py`. The `penetration_gap` headline reads `vessel_wall_death_rate`, which `drug_transport` evaluates at `r = 0`, where `C(r) = vessel_wall_conc * vascular_permeability * exp(-r/lambda)` loses its `lambda` term exactly. This measures what that does to the published verdict label.

## 1. Two drugs, two penetration lengths, one answer

| drug | lambda (um) | Epithelial (well-vascularized) wall kill | Epithelial (poorly-vascularized) wall kill | Neuroectodermal (CNS/BBB) wall kill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RSL3-like | 100.0 | 0.121000 | 0.026000 | 0.018000 |
| Doxorubicin-transport | 50.0 | 0.121000 | 0.026000 | 0.018000 |

The two arms differ 2.0-fold in penetration length and agree **bit-for-bit** at every tissue. The headline observable therefore carries no information about penetration; what orders the three numbers is `vascular_permeability` (0.8, 0.4, 0.15).

This is the parameter split that matters: #335 validated `lambda` against measured data (Primeau 2005, Tannock 2002) and `CALIBRATION_STATUS.md` records it; `vascular_permeability` carries no anchor in this repository. The headline uses only the unanchored one.

## 2. The three numbers are one dose-response at three points

| tissue | c0 | binary wall kill | `sim_batch` at c0 | exact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Epithelial (well-vascularized) | 0.8 | 0.121000 | 0.121000 | yes |
| Epithelial (poorly-vascularized) | 0.4 | 0.026000 | 0.026000 | yes |
| Neuroectodermal (CNS/BBB) | 0.15 | 0.018000 | 0.018000 | yes |

`cell_seed` in `sim-tissue-pk` is `seed + bin_idx * N_CELLS * 2 + i * 2`, which does not depend on the tissue, so at bin 0 the three tissues run identical cells with identical RNG and differ in nothing but `c0`. Reproducing each wall kill exactly from `sim_batch(seed=42, n=1000)` confirms it.

That dose-response is monotone non-decreasing across all 21 grid points (**yes**), and the well >= poorly >= CNS ordering held in 200/200 prior draws. Those two facts are the same fact: a paired comparison of one monotone function at a fixed decreasing input sequence cannot invert, so the ordering is robust to the rate constants **by construction** and the draw count is not evidence about them.

## 3. Most of the CNS number is the no-drug control

| tissue | headline | no-drug control | drug-attributable | fold over control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Epithelial (well-vascularized) | 0.121 | 0.014 | +0.107 | 8.64x |
| Epithelial (poorly-vascularized) | 0.026 | 0.014 | +0.012 | 1.86x |
| Neuroectodermal (CNS/BBB) | 0.018 | 0.014 | +0.004 | 1.29x |

`Control` and `RSL3` at zero inhibition are the same number (0.014), so the right-hand column is what the drug adds. Read against its own control, the gradient is far steeper than the headline triple suggests at the well-vascularized end and far shallower at the CNS end, where the drug contributes +0.004. In 27 of 200 prior draws the CNS kill is not even strictly above that draw's own control.

## 4. Access and distance disagree about which tissue is worst

| tissue | vascular permeability (access) | inter-vessel half-distance um (distance) |
|---|---|---|
| Epithelial (well-vascularized) | 0.8 | 60.0 |
| Epithelial (poorly-vascularized) | 0.4 | 125.0 |
| Neuroectodermal (CNS/BBB) | 0.15 | 75.0 |

Ranked by access: Epithelial (well-vascularized) > Epithelial (poorly-vascularized) > Neuroectodermal (CNS/BBB). Ranked by proximity to a vessel: Epithelial (well-vascularized) < Neuroectodermal (CNS/BBB) < Epithelial (poorly-vascularized). The two orders DISAGREE, and the published ordering is the access one -- measured at the one radius where distance cannot act.

| drug | lambda (um) | deepest-cell conc, Epithelial (poorly-vascularized) | deepest-cell conc, Neuroectodermal (CNS/BBB) | poorly/CNS order |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RSL3-like | 100.0 | 0.1146 | 0.0709 | poorly > CNS |
| Doxorubicin-transport | 50.0 | 0.0328 | 0.0335 | CNS > poorly (INVERTED) |

Solving the two profiles for equality gives a crossover at **lambda = 51.0 um**, derived from the stored presets and then CHECKED against the engine: the two shipped lambdas must fall on opposite sides of it and the measured concentrations must invert on exactly the predicted side (**confirmed**). It falls inside Tannock 2002's measured 25-75 um band, while the 100 um RSL3-like value that preserves the published ordering at depth is the one `CALIBRATION_STATUS.md` flags as unvalidated.

**The inversion is in the concentration, not the kill.** At those depths both arms sit far below the bistable threshold and their kills are indistinguishable (0.009 and 0.009 at lambda 50), so no kill ordering flips. What flips is the transport quantity the word 'penetration' refers to.

## Verdict

`direction_robust_by_construction_magnitude_not`

The ordering claim is not false -- it is robust to the 11 rate constants, here in every draw. It is robust **by construction**, and it describes vascular access rather than penetration. `identifiability_report.py` derives the `penetration_gap` row from this artifact so the label cannot outrun the measurement again.

## Scope

- Nothing is recalibrated. `vascular_permeability` has no published anchor in this repository, and supplying one from memory would be worse than naming it uncalibrated.
- The manuscript's Section 6.1 treatment is CORRECT and untouched: it attributes the numbers to vascular permeability, names the vessel wall as the highest-concentration position, and calls the transport parameters estimated. Only the Chapter 5 sentence inherited from the uncertainty generator is corrected.
- The two drugs differ in transport only; the cell-level pharmacology is the RSL3/GPX4 pathway in both arms, as `sim-tissue-pk` states.
- The deepest-bin comparison is at each tissue's OWN half-distance, which is the worst-perfused cell it contains, not a common depth. At a common depth the shared lambda cancels from the comparison too and the access ordering returns.
