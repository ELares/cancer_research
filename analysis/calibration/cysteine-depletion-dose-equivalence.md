# What a cyst(e)inase is worth in erastin, and what the engine will not say

Generated by `scripts/cysteine_depletion_dose_equivalence.py`. No layer is added here; every parameter used is one the engine already carries.

## Dose equivalence, which needs no simulation

The #909 map turns a residual cystine concentration into a supply fraction (Km 179.4 uM, reference 207.7 uM). The committed erastin fit turns a dose into the same quantity (`dose^6/(dose^6 + 6^6)`, so 6 uM is the half-maximal dose, RMSE 0.0697, Glycolytic). Composing them is arithmetic over published constants.

| cystine removed | residual (uM) | supply cut | erastin equivalent |
| --: | --: | --: | --: |
| 25.0% | 155.8 | 13.4% | 4.40 uM |
| 50.0% | 103.8 | 31.7% | 5.28 uM |
| 75.0% | 51.9 | 58.2% | 6.34 uM |
| 90.0% | 20.8 | 80.7% | 7.61 uM |
| 95.0% | 10.4 | 89.8% | 8.62 uM |
| 99.0% | 2.1 | 97.9% | 11.35 uM |

The enzyme's whole operating range lands in **4.40-11.35 uM** erastin, a **2.6x** window, every point inside the calibrated dose grid (0.1-30.0 uM). That narrowness is the Hill coefficient of 6, not a property of the enzyme: a steep switch compresses a wide range of supply cuts into a short stretch of dose.

## A structural ceiling

The enzyme cannot cut supply below zero, so **through this mechanism it cannot exceed a saturating erastin dose**. That is the engine's topology rather than a measurement -- both routes enter through the same supply factor -- and it is the useful kind of negative. The advantage PMID 39696900 claims, "efficacy, persistence, and precision", has to come from targeting and persistence, because mechanistic depth is already spent by a drug that exists.

**And it is not only an argument.** At the top of the committed dose grid (30 uM) the Hill map already sits at 99.994% inhibition -- written to three decimals because `pct` rounds it to a flat 100% and it is not 100% -- so the committed saturating-erastin increment IS a complete-block increment measured independently of this page. It is 0.4215; a complete cystine block here gives 0.4288, agreeing to 0.0073 absolute. That does double duty: the ceiling is confirmed, and this page is confirmed to drive the engine the same way `calibrate_erastin.py` did -- a harness disagreeing with the committed numbers would be measuring something else and every figure above would be suspect.

## The transsulfuration escape is an exact no-op switch

`xc_cystine_factor()` is `1 - erastin_xc_inhib * (1 - transsulfuration_floor)`, so the supply actually removed is `inhib * (1 - floor)` and the fraction delivered is `1 - floor` -- independent of the inhibition entirely.

| transsulfuration floor | nominal cut delivered | factor at a COMPLETE block |
| --: | --: | --: |
| 0.0% | 100.0% | 0.000 |
| 25.0% | 75.0% | 0.250 |
| 50.0% | 50.0% | 0.500 |
| 75.0% | 25.0% | 0.750 |
| 100.0% | 0.0% | 1.000 |

At a floor of 1 the factor is **exactly 1 for every inhibition** (`True`): a fully competent transsulfuration pathway makes the entire cystine arm a precise no-op, at any depletion depth and at any cascade. This is the quantitative reason the second bacterial paper (PMID 41704298) had to generate H2S to inhibit the bypass, and it needs no calibration to state.

## What this refuses to say

**No absolute kill rate.** Not "not yet measured" -- refused, because both available cascades fail in opposite directions and the repo already has the gate that catches it (baseline viability 2.0%, from `headline-at-fitted-cascade.json`).

| cascade | untreated Glycolytic | worst untreated phenotype | admissible | complete block |
| --- | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| `in_vitro_fitted` | 8.9% | 100.0% | False | 51.8% |
| `in_vivo_default` | 0.0% | 1.1% | True | 0.0% |

The in-vitro fitted cascade reproduces the real CTRPv2 erastin curve and the arm reaches 51.8% at a complete block -- but its untreated Glycolytic control dies at 8.9%, over the 2.0% constraint, and its worst untreated phenotype at 100.0%. The admissible in-vivo default has every control at or near zero and a complete block kills 0.0% -- the arm does nothing.

So the magnitude of this arm is not predictable by this engine today, for a reason already on record rather than a new one: the same in-vitro/in-vivo cascade disjunction `headline_at_fitted.py` published for every other headline. A FIRST DRAFT OF THIS PAGE REPORTED THE FITTED-CASCADE KILL TABLE AS A RESULT. Running the untreated controls is what stopped it, and that check is the only reason this section exists.

The no-op identity is also confirmed by measurement rather than left as algebra: at a full floor a complete block reproduces the untreated rate EXACTLY at both cascades (True, True).

## What would unblock the magnitude

A cascade that is admissible AND reproduces an inducer dose-response. That is the standing `#500` problem, not a cysteine-specific one. The cysteine-specific piece is cheaper and named in `cysteine-depletion-feasibility.md`: the transporter Km measured in a pancreatic line, since the only kinetic analysis in the cancer literature is a single lung-cell-line paper.
