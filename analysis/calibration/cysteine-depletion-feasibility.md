## The target: a measured Km and a formulation constant

- cystine-uptake Km **179.4 +/- 26.7 uM** (PMID 34287957, A549)
- RPMI-1640 reference **207.7 uM** (65.0 mg/L L-cystine 2HCl / 313.0 g/mol), a formulation constant, not a measurement

Medium therefore sits at **53.7%** of saturation -- just past the knee. Nothing in the map is chosen, so it can fail.

**One cell line is not a shortcut, it is the literature.** Of 228 records naming cystine uptake, only 2 report kinetic constants, and one of those two studies cultured fibroblasts in cystinuria and Hartnup disease (PMID 5054467, dated in the landmark table rather than here) -- not a tumour. So in cancer cell lines there is exactly ONE kinetic analysis across 7,727,217 records, which is what PMID 34287957 says of itself: very few reports exist. 7 records pair cystine uptake with pancreas and NONE of them is kinetic (they measure expression, drug response or isotope tracing). That makes measuring this Km in a PDAC line a genuinely novel experiment rather than a tidying-up exercise, and it is the single cheapest thing that would tighten this page.

What the map assumes, stated because these are where it would break before the arithmetic does (no count in this sentence: a spelled one goes stale the moment another assumption is added):

0. **The map sets `erastin_xc_inhib` alone.** `xc_cystine_factor()` is `1 - erastin_xc_inhib * (1 - transsulfuration_floor)`, so the figures below equal the engine's supply factor exactly only at a zero floor; a non-zero floor adds supply back on top, which is correct because the floor is a SEPARATE source (methionine, not import) and not part of the transporter curve. Set the two independently and do not count the floor twice.
1. **Glutathione resynthesis is proportional to cystine uptake.** This is the engine's own assumption, not one added here -- #502 made the NRF2-driven resynthesis term *be* the cystine supply -- but it is the step that lets a transporter rate stand in for a supply fraction, so it is the first thing to doubt if the map disagrees with an assay.
2. **The transporter is the rate limit.** Macropinocytosis is a parallel route to cysteine that an enzyme in the medium does not touch (PMID 35287706, where it mitigates sorafenib-induced ferroptosis), and the map is blind to it. A cell line using it would be depleted on paper and supplied in fact.
3. **An initial-rate constant is being used as a steady-state one.** The Km comes from a 5-min linear-uptake window; the engine applies the factor every step of a long run. Nothing here measures whether the constant holds once the intracellular pool has drawn down.

## Depletion buys less supply reduction than its percentage suggests

| cystine removed | residual (uM) | supply cut it is worth | marginal return |
| --: | --: | --: | --: |
| 25.0% | 155.8 | 13.4% | 0.62 |
| 50.0% | 103.8 | 31.7% | 0.87 |
| 75.0% | 51.9 | 58.2% | 1.30 |
| 90.0% | 20.8 | 80.7% | 1.73 |
| 95.0% | 10.4 | 89.8% | 1.93 |
| 99.0% | 2.1 | 97.9% | 2.11 |

Removing **50.0%** of the cystine is worth an `erastin_xc_inhib` of only **31.7%**. The marginal return starts at **0.46** -- `Km/(Km+S0)`, necessarily under one wherever there is substrate -- crosses one at **59.5%** depletion and ends at **2.16**, so the last substrate removed is worth several times the first. That is the opposite of a saturating drug. The quantity that matters is therefore how close to zero the concentration is HELD, not the average depletion, which is a mechanistic argument for a continuously producing living source over a bolus enzyme.

| supply cut wanted | depletion required | residual (uM) |
| --: | --: | --: |
| 25.0% | 41.8% | 120.8 |
| 50.0% | 68.3% | 65.8 |
| 75.0% | 86.6% | 27.8 |
| 90.0% | 95.1% | 10.2 |

A 90.0% supply cut needs 95.1% depletion, down to 10.2 uM. That is the quantitative content of PMID 39696900's own complaint that existing methods fail to block cysteine metabolism efficiently.

### The gap is a CULTURE phenomenon, and that qualifies the headline

Everything above is measured against a medium reference ABOVE the Km (207.7 against 179.4 uM), which is why the curve is flat where the first substrate is removed. Well BELOW the Km the same curve is almost linear and depletion maps nearly one-to-one. Taking the committed free-cysteine figure of 8.2 uM as a low reference, 50.0% depletion is worth **48.9%** instead of **31.7%**, and a 90.0% supply cut needs **90.4%** depletion instead of **95.1%**.

That figure is an ILLUSTRATION of the regime effect, not a claim about the in-vivo map: it is free CYSTEINE, not free cystine, and the transporter imports cystine. The point that survives is structural -- the in-vitro regime the engine's erastin fit lives in and the in-vivo regime an enzyme therapy acts in sit on OPPOSITE sides of the transporter's half-saturation point, so a fraction fitted in culture does not carry across unexamined. Establishing the free-cystine concentration in tumour interstitium is what would close this, and nothing here does.

## How much rides on the Km

One cell line (A549, lung) in one paper, against a pancreatic target, so the scale is the weak half of the anchor. The SHAPE survives; the depletion a given cut requires does move:

| Km case | Km (uM) | saturation at reference | initial marginal return | depletion for a 90.0% cut |
| --- | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| `km_low` | 152.7 | 57.6% | 0.42 | 95.5% |
| `km_point` | 179.4 | 53.7% | 0.46 | 95.1% |
| `km_high` | 206.1 | 50.2% | 0.50 | 94.8% |
| `km_half` | 89.7 | 69.8% | 0.30 | 96.8% |
| `km_double` | 358.8 | 36.7% | 0.63 | 93.4% |

Across a doubling and a halving of the Km the required depletion spans 93.4%-96.8% -- so the conclusion that deep depletion is needed is robust to the constant, while any specific figure is not. Measuring the Km in a pancreatic line is the cheap next step and would need no new model code.

## What is NOT expressible, and it is wiring rather than data

- `transsulfuration_floor` is a `Params` field: True
- it has a per-cell `Cell` counterpart: True
- `cell.nrf2` is a per-cell field: True
- glutathione resynthesis sites in `biochem.rs`: 2, of which 0 are multiplied by `xc_cystine_factor()` and 2 by `cell.nrf2`

Because EVERY resynthesis site is multiplied by the cystine factor, a complete block is zero at every NRF2 level (`nrf2_cannot_rescue_complete_block`: False). That is the right topology -- transsulfuration is the only cysteine source once import is gone -- and it is also why raising `cell.nrf2` is not a substitute for a per-cell escape.

This matters because of a tension in the paper's own design. PMID 41422256 reports that hypoxia activates the transsulfuration pathway and so confers ferroptosis resistance under cystine limitation, and these bacteria are hypoxia-TRIGGERED: the trigger co-locates with the escape. The second bacterial paper, PMID 41704298, independently added H2S to inhibit the transsulfuration bypass -- which is the one escape this parameter structure leaves open. Read that as a CORRESPONDENCE, not a prediction: both papers were in hand before the structure was consulted, and nothing here was forecast in advance. The engine cannot state it today, because the hypoxia field is per-cell and the escape is global. Moving the floor to `Cell` is a wiring change carrying a byte-identity obligation, not a missing dataset.

## Verdict

- the supply arm is **already expressible**, with no new axis, via `erastin_xc_inhib` plus `transsulfuration_floor`
- the concentration-to-fraction map has a **named, failable target** (Km 179.4 uM, PMID 34287957; reference 207.7 uM by formulation)
- a hypoxia-graded escape is **not expressible**: a per-cell floor is missing
- the depletion-buys-less headline is **in-vitro only**: the gap exists because medium sits above the Km, and below the Km the map is nearly linear, so the in-vitro fit and the in-vivo therapy sit on opposite sides of half-saturation
- the dose magnitude stays **unanchored** -- nothing here ties enzyme activity to a residual concentration in vivo, and the plasma pools in the constants table are TOTAL cyst(e)ine and free cysteine, neither of which is free cystine, so they cannot serve as the in-vivo reference
