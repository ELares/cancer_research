# From first literature to first trial

**Headline: the measurement mostly does not work, and why it does not is the useful part.** A census can in principle date a mechanism's literature and its first trial; doing so needs a vocabulary that is precise at its EARLIEST match, and neither instrument here is. The table is published with every row flagged for which failure applies to it.

Generated by `scripts/census_translation_lag.py` over 4,403,994 census records. A mechanism STARTS in its first year with an article and its first trial is the first year an article carries an NLM clinical-trial publication type. Both use the SAME threshold, which is what makes the lag a duration: a trial record is also an article record, so at a matched threshold the first trial cannot precede the first article. Years after 2025 are excluded as incomplete.

Every mechanism is measured twice. The MeSH arm uses expert-assigned descriptors and is blind to anything before the descriptor existed; the text arm uses this project's keyword vocabulary over title and abstract only. **The gap between the two start years is a measurement of MeSH's own history, not of the field**, and it is reported per mechanism rather than assumed uniform.

## What the two arms disagree about

The **arm gap** is the MeSH start minus the text start, and it is NOT a measure of when a descriptor was minted -- an earlier draft called it that and the sign refuted it. A POSITIVE gap means the text sees the concept first, which is the descriptor-introduction effect. A NEGATIVE gap means the DESCRIPTOR is older or broader than the term: `Ultrasonic Therapy` runs from the 1950s while the word "sonodynamic" is recent, so the descriptor arm starts decades earlier and is counting something wider. The column mixes both effects, which is why it is named for what it measures rather than for what it was meant to measure. Median 1.5 years; read the per-mechanism column, not the median.

6 mechanism(s) start EARLIER on the descriptor arm: `antibody-drug-conjugate`, `car-t`, `epigenetic`, `metabolic-targeting`, `sonodynamic`, `synthetic-lethality`. For these the descriptor is the broader instrument and its lag is measured over a wider literature than the term names.

**Fragility** is how many years later the start moves if a mechanism must reach 5 articles rather than 1. A large value means one early match is carrying the start year, so the lag beside it is an upper bound on the true duration.

**Only 4 of 25 mechanisms support the measurement at all.** Their median lag from first literature to first indexed trial is 9.0 years: `bispecific-antibody`, `cold-atmospheric-plasma`, `nanoparticle`, `phagocytosis-checkpoint`. The median over all 25 is 16 years and should not be quoted -- it averages durations together with numbers set by a single early false positive.

That 4-of-25 IS the result. The question is answerable in principle from a census and is not answerable with the vocabulary this project has, and the reason is specific enough to act on.

The other 21 have a start year carried by one early match. Their lags are UPPER BOUNDS -- a false positive sets the start EARLIER than the truth, which makes the computed lag LONGER, so the real duration is shorter than the number shown. (An earlier draft said lower bounds, reasoning from the direction of the error without carrying it through the subtraction.) The mechanisms: `antibody-drug-conjugate`, `bioelectric`, `car-t`, `crispr`, `cuproptosis`, `disulfidptosis`, `electrochemical-therapy`, `electrolysis`, `epigenetic`, `frequency-therapy`, `hifu`, `immunotherapy`, `metabolic-targeting`, `microbiome`, `mrna-vaccine`, `oncolytic-virus`, `radioligand-therapy`, `sonodynamic`, `synthetic-lethality`, `targeted-protein-degradation`, `ttfields`.

## Two instrument defects found and fixed, and one that remains

A first version dated `car-t` to **1947** and `electrolysis` to **1950**. Both were unbounded substring matches: `car t` fires inside "s(car t)issue" and `echt` inside "li(echt)enstein". Word boundaries moved `car-t` to 2000, a 53-year correction. A first version also used a 5-article threshold for the start and a 1-trial threshold for the event, which put trials BEFORE their own literature -- TTFields showed a 2007 trial against a 2013 start. Matching the thresholds makes the ordering hold by construction, since a trial record is also an article record.

WHAT REMAINS IS POLYSEMY, which no boundary fixes. `electrolysis` still starts in 1952 on a paper about cosmetic hair removal and iris cysts -- a real use of the word, a different subject. `cuproptosis` starts in 1982 on `copper ionophore`, a genuine term applied to disulfiram four decades before cuproptosis was named. Those rows are wrong in a way the fragility column flags but does not repair.

The general point is worth more than the table. **A first-appearance statistic has no error averaging.** A prevalence estimate from an 82.5%-precise vocabulary is off by a predictable fraction; a MINIMUM computed from the same vocabulary is decided entirely by its single worst false positive across four million records. The same instrument supports one statistic and not the other.

## What a lag is not

It is not time to approval, and it is not evidence that a mechanism translated WELL -- an indexed trial says a trial happened, not that it worked, and a fast lag can mean a low barrier to a first-in-human study rather than a strong result. The text arm also carries this project's keyword vocabulary, whose mechanism precision is 82.5%, so an early stray match can start the clock early -- which is what the fragility column measures per mechanism rather than assuming a single threshold fixes it.
