# From first literature to first trial

A census can date the earliest indexed literature and trial matched by each vocabulary. Interpreting those dates needs a vocabulary that is precise at its EARLIEST match. The table reports censoring and sensitivity to the article-count threshold; these checks do not establish the accuracy of individual matches.

Generated by `scripts/census_translation_lag.py` over 4,403,994 census records. A mechanism STARTS in its first year with an article and its first trial is the first year an article carries an NLM clinical-trial publication type. Both use the SAME threshold, which is what makes the lag a duration: a trial record is also an article record, so at a matched threshold the first trial cannot precede the first article. Years after 2025 are excluded as incomplete.

Mechanisms are assessed on two arms where the relevant vocabulary is available. The MeSH arm uses expert-assigned descriptors; the text arm uses this project's keyword vocabulary over title and abstract only. **The gap between the two start years measures their observed first-match ordering.** It does not isolate descriptor history, vocabulary coverage or match errors, and it is reported per mechanism rather than assumed uniform.

## What the two arms disagree about

The **arm gap** is the MeSH start minus the text start, and it is NOT a measure of when a descriptor was minted. A POSITIVE gap means the earliest text match precedes the earliest descriptor match; a NEGATIVE gap means the reverse. Descriptor introduction or breadth could contribute, as could vocabulary coverage and match errors. The sign alone does not identify the cause; attributing it requires source-level checks of the matches and descriptor history. Median 1.5 years; read the per-mechanism column, not the median.

6 mechanism(s) start EARLIER on the descriptor arm: `antibody-drug-conjugate`, `car-t`, `epigenetic`, `metabolic-targeting`, `sonodynamic`, `synthetic-lethality`. This records the observed ordering; it does not establish that these descriptors cover broader literature than the text vocabulary.

**Fragility** is how many years later the start moves if a mechanism must reach 5 articles rather than 1. A large value measures sensitivity to this threshold. It does not identify false matches or bound a true duration without checking both the first literature and first trial matches.

**Only 4 of 25 mechanisms with text-arm lags meet the declared stability screen.** Their median lag from first literature to first indexed trial is 9.0 years: `bispecific-antibody`, `cold-atmospheric-plasma`, `nanoparticle`, `phagocytosis-checkpoint`.

The median over all 25 is 16 years and should not be quoted without the stability qualification: it includes lags with unstable or unavailable threshold comparisons. That alone does not identify false-positive matches.

For 21 mechanisms, the start moves by more than 5 years at the stability threshold. Their observed lags are threshold-sensitive, not established bounds on time to translation. The mechanisms: `antibody-drug-conjugate`, `bioelectric`, `car-t`, `crispr`, `cuproptosis`, `disulfidptosis`, `electrochemical-therapy`, `electrolysis`, `epigenetic`, `frequency-therapy`, `hifu`, `immunotherapy`, `metabolic-targeting`, `microbiome`, `mrna-vaccine`, `oncolytic-virus`, `radioligand-therapy`, `sonodynamic`, `synthetic-lethality`, `targeted-protein-degradation`, `ttfields`.

## Two instrument defects found and fixed, and one that remains

A first version dated `car-t` to **1947** and `electrolysis` to **1950**. Both were unbounded substring matches: `car t` fires inside "s(car t)issue" and `echt` inside "li(echt)enstein". Word boundaries moved `car-t` to 2000, a 53-year correction. A first version also used a 5-article threshold for the start and a 1-trial threshold for the event, which put trials BEFORE their own literature -- TTFields showed a 2007 trial against a 2013 start. Matching the thresholds makes the ordering hold by construction, since a trial record is also an article record.

WHAT REMAINS IS POLYSEMY, which no boundary fixes. In the earlier full census analysis, `electrolysis` started in 1952 on a paper about cosmetic hair removal and iris cysts -- a real use of the word, a different subject. `cuproptosis` started in 1982 on `copper ionophore`, a genuine term applied to disulfiram four decades before cuproptosis was named. These historical examples required source-level checks; threshold sensitivity alone does not establish that a match is wrong.

The general point is worth more than the table. **A first-appearance statistic has no error averaging.** Aggregate label counts depend on both precision and recall; the vocabulary's measured 82.5% precision alone does not establish the accuracy of prevalence estimates. A false positive can move a first-appearance year earlier, while missed matches can move it later. Threshold stability does not establish the accuracy of either date.

## What a lag is not

It is not time to approval, and it is not evidence that a mechanism translated WELL -- an indexed trial says a trial happened, not that it worked, and a fast lag can mean a low barrier to a first-in-human study rather than a strong result. The fragility column measures sensitivity to the article-count threshold, not whether individual matches are correct. Both stable and threshold-sensitive lags need source-level verification of the first literature and trial matches.
