# Per-mechanism growth at census scale

Generated by `scripts/census_mechanism_growth.py` over 4,403,994 census records, 2015 to 2025, with mechanisms labelled by MeSH descriptor rather than by keyword.

## The denominator a growth claim needs

| | 2015 | 2025 | growth |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| cancer literature (census) | 133,204 | 147,008 | x1.1 |
| **articles carrying any mechanism descriptor** | 6,050 | 16,776 | **x2.77** |

The mechanisms this project tracks grew **x2.52** faster than cancer literature as a whole. That is a real result and it is the part of the manuscript's growth story that survives: these are not simply riding a rising tide, because there is no rising tide -- the field is close to flat.

It is also the denominator `manuscript_vs_census.py` should have used and did not. Comparing the retrieved corpus against the whole field attributes ALL of the corpus's rise to the mechanisms, when the mechanisms account for x2.77 of it. A corpus built from queries about emerging therapies outgrows all of cancer research whether or not anything unusual happened; the question a growth claim is asking is whether it outgrew the thing it is about.

## What this does not say

Growth in indexed articles is growth in attention, not in evidence, results or clinical progress. MeSH indexing also lags publication, so the 2025 column is under-counted and every ratio here is a lower bound; the lag applies to numerator and denominator alike, so the comparison between rows is less affected than any single row.
