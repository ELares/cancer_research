# Ferroptosis as a mechanism of normal-tissue harm

Generated by `scripts/census_normal_tissue.py`. Issue #728 records this project's selectivity premise -- that ferroptosis inducers are attractive because normal cells resist them -- and notes that *toxicity* appears once in the manuscript. Across the census, **575 of the 13,346 articles carrying the `Ferroptosis` descriptor (4.31%) also carry an organ-toxicity descriptor**, and on a hand-read sample those are papers in which ferroptosis is the mechanism by which normal tissue is damaged. Where they sit in the census is the second finding, and it is why this project has not met them.

## Where this literature sits, and why the project has not met it

**558 of the 575 enter the census through the ADJACENT extension and 17 through the C04 cancer tree.** As a rate: the ferroptosis literature indexed as cancer touches organ toxicity in 0.33% of its articles against 6.79% of the adjacent stream, a factor of 20.6.

**That gap is mostly a labelling effect and should not be read as neglect.** A paper about doxorubicin injuring a heart is indexed under `Cardiotoxicity` and `Doxorubicin`, not under a `Neoplasms` descriptor, because its subject is the heart. The consequence for this project holds either way: a corpus scoped to cancer will not surface the literature bearing on its own selectivity premise, whatever the reason it sits elsewhere.

It also corrects this analysis's own first framing, which called these papers organ damage in cancer patients receiving cancer drugs. A third mention a cancer drug or therapy at all, and the adjudicated sample holds cadmium in Nile tilapia and a herbicide in mice. The cancer-drug core below is real; it is not the whole set.

## Direction is adjudicated, not matched

109 of these articles (19.0%) name a ferroptosis INHIBITOR -- ferrostatin-1, liproxstatin-1, deferoxamine. **That count cannot be read as papers proposing to block ferroptosis to protect an organ.** Fer-1 rescue is the field's standard specificity control, and this project's own P1 protocol uses it that way, so one string serves two opposite roles.

So a sample of 40 was read (`analysis/normal-tissue-adjudication.csv`, seed 728), and the verdicts are the measurement:

| verdict | records |
|---|--:|
| harm | 38 |
| review | 2 |

**The predicted confound did not appear, and its direction was predicted wrong.** Of the 5 sampled records naming an inhibitor, 0 use it as a bare specificity probe: in an organ-injury paper the inhibitor IS the protective intervention. So the keyword arm does not overstate the protective literature as this analysis first warned -- it understates it, since 38 of 40 records read as ferroptosis-mediated injury while only 5 name an inhibitor at all. Knowing that a measure errs is not knowing which way.

The uniformity is itself the result. An adjudication that finds one direction in 38 of 40 records is weak evidence about a contested question and strong evidence about an uncontested one, and this question is uncontested inside this literature: these papers agree that normal kidney, liver, heart and neural tissue die by ferroptosis under stress.

## What this does and does not establish

**It does not refute the selectivity premise.** A literature showing normal tissue can die by ferroptosis under a chemotherapeutic does not establish that a targeted GPX4 inhibitor at a therapeutic dose would harm it, and the two claims are easy to confuse. Cisplatin and doxorubicin injure kidneys and hearts through several mechanisms at once; ferroptosis being among them says nothing directly about RSL3.

**What it establishes is that the premise is an open empirical question with a literature attached, rather than a background fact.** That is the difference between a caveat and an assumption, and it is the gap issue #728 identifies: the project scores every modality on kill and none on harm, and the one compartment it does model is a cancer-associated fibroblast, which is tumour-resident and not normal tissue at all.

## For the layer-freeze policy

A normal-tissue phenotype is the addition #728 asks for, and CONTRIBUTING.md requires a named calibration target before one lands. This analysis does not supply it: an article count is not a dose-response, and the organ-toxicity literature reports histology and biomarkers rather than the matched normal-versus-tumour kill curves a phenotype would be fitted to. The committed CTRPv2 curves are cancer cell lines only. So the target remains unnamed, and the honest position is that the selectivity assumption should be stated as one in the manuscript before it is modelled.
