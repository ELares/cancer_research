# How the literature splits on hypoxia and ferroptosis

Generated by `scripts/census_hypoxia_direction.py`. 291 census articles carry `Ferroptosis` together with a hypoxia-family descriptor (`anoxia`, `cell hypoxia`, `hypoxia`, `hypoxia-inducible factor 1`, `hypoxia-inducible factor 1, alpha subunit`, `tumor hypoxia`). Taken as a union rather than one descriptor, because no single term carries this literature and picking one would be picking a result.

Section 7.1 reports a pharmacologic ferroptosis inducer collapsing under simulated hypoxia while sonodynamic therapy is far less affected, and flags the leg as genuinely contested. **Contested is itself a claim about the literature**, and it is checkable: if the field reports one direction nine times in ten, the word is wrong and the manuscript is hedging where it need not.

| framing | articles | share of all |
|---|--:|--:|
| hypoxia PROTECTS against ferroptosis | 12 | 4% |
| hypoxia SENSITISES to ferroptosis | 20 | 7% |
| both directions present | 5 | 2% |
| neither pattern matched | 254 | 87% |

## The keyword classifier failed, and the failure is instructive

All 32 articles the classifier labelled were adjudicated from their titles. It agrees **34.4%** of the time and REVERSES the direction in **7** cases -- 5 of them cancer-context, which is the subset that matters here.

The mechanism is exact, and no amount of pattern-tuning fixes it: **"hypoxia-induced ferroptosis resistance" CONTAINS "hypoxia-induced ferroptosis"**. A phrase asserting that hypoxia PROTECTS contains, as a substring, the phrase asserting that it SENSITISES. Proximity cannot separate them, and the construction is common precisely in tumour biology, where "ferroptosis resistance" is the thing being studied.

So the regex is demoted to generating candidates and the adjudication is the measurement. **It reverses the answer**: the classifier reported the sensitising direction leading 62% to 38%, and the adjudication puts the PROTECTIVE direction ahead 61.1% to 39% over 18 directional articles (95% Wilson interval 38.6-79.7%).

That interval is wide enough to contain an even split, so what this establishes is that BOTH directions are reported in substantial numbers and NOT their ratio. Section 7.1's 'contested' framing therefore reflects the literature. The protective direction -- the one this project's simulation assumes for its pharmacologic arm -- is the one that leads on the adjudicated count, but leads by less than the uncertainty.

12 of 32 are off-topic altogether (a drug's effect in a hypoxic context, rather than hypoxia's own), and 2 state no direction in the title. Those are excluded rather than assigned, which is why the directional denominator is 18 and not 32.

## Limits of the adjudication

Titles only, one adjudicator, and 18 directional articles. That is weaker evidence than a working classifier at scale would be. It is stronger than the classifier actually is, which was the choice available -- and every label is committed in `analysis/hypoxia-direction-adjudication.csv` with its reason, so a reader can disagree with any of them individually.

## What this cannot do

Settle the biology. Hypoxia's effect on ferroptosis genuinely differs by cell type, by inducer, and by which arm of the pathway a study perturbs, so a majority direction is not the answer and an even split is not confusion. Both are facts about what has been REPORTED. The manuscript's simulation work is what bounds the conditions, and this measures only whether the word attached to it describes the literature.

89.0% of the intersection matches neither pattern or both, so the split is over the classified remainder. The patterns were fixed before any result was read and are not adjusted afterwards: this project's simulation ASSUMES the protective direction for its pharmacologic arm, so a vocabulary widened after seeing the split would be fitted to the answer the project has already built.
